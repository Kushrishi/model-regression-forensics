## Question and existing evidence

Can a release debugger distinguish a sufficiently supported unique explanation from ambiguity or insufficient evidence, given versioned changes and bounded intervention evidence? M4 does not answer this. In two constructed Banking77 worlds, label overlap and lexical Jaccard rank the root first in both; Grad-Dot and TracIn rank it first/fifth. Three paired trajectories per world are not six independent incidents. Candidate semantics expose a shortcut. Two different repairs can restore behavior without establishing unique historical responsibility.

The current artifact is a bounded reproducible negative case study. M4 localization is closed. Its results do not justify scaling or a new certification experiment.

## Prior work and possible boundary

See the [continuation dossier](CONTINUATION_DOSSIER_2026_10.md) for primary sources and access limitations. Influence Functions, TracIn, Datamodels, TRAK and DataInf already address attribution; Datascope addresses pipeline-aware debugging; distributional attribution addresses training randomness. DeMix and counterfactual-specification work further narrow the space. Selective prediction supplies established risk/coverage concepts. None of those ingredients alone is a contribution. The [7 October dossier update](CONTINUATION_DOSSIER_2026_10.md#7-october-primary-source-review-update) adds group-interaction diagnostics under a compute budget and existing pipeline what-if tooling: these also cannot be claimed as new. The remaining release-level decision distinction still needs independent judgment.

The possible distinct target is a practical incident decision: investigate/revert one candidate, test several, or gather more evidence while avoiding incorrect specific attribution. The simplest equal-evidence baseline is release-diff/semantic prioritization followed by the same paired interventions and retrainings. If that workflow solves the decision equally well, close the research branch.

## Why another benchmark might or might not help

A benchmark could expose overlapping changes, multiple restorative interventions, missing candidates and interactions that M4 does not exercise. It would be a bad idea if ambiguity is artificially manufactured, truth is encoded in metadata, direct inspection solves incidents, or it merely rewards thresholding an existing attribution score. This packet specifies no construction, numerical threshold, seed cohort or result-bearing protocol.

## Substrates and cost

Existing DistilBERT is the cheapest continuity baseline. SmolLM2-135M-Instruct is a provisional open instruction-model candidate; Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct is a larger alternative. A structured, machine-checkable response task is preferable to subjective free-text grading. Modern scale is justified only by the decision, not portfolio appeal. No model has been selected or downloaded for continuation.

Illustration, not an approved cohort: S paired seeds and K interventions require S(2+K) fits per incident. S=3, K=5, four incidents imply 84 fits; assumed 10–60 minutes/fit imply 14–84 accelerator-hours. These are unmeasured estimates. Raw fp32 final weights for 84 × 135M models are approximately 45 GB, before optimizer/checkpoint storage.

## Questions the independent reviewer must answer

1. Is selective release-level diagnosis distinct from attribution, data debugging and counterfactual specification?
2. Is incorrect unique attribution a useful engineering target with a defensible truth definition?
3. Do several plausible interventions represent real debugging, or an artificial puzzle?
4. Does a simple equal-evidence intervention workflow already solve the intended decision?
5. Is modern-model scaling scientifically necessary or only portfolio motivated?
6. Can incomplete candidate sets and interaction causes be evaluated honestly?
7. Should MRF close as a completed negative/reproducible case study?

## Recommendation and kill criteria

Close continuation if its benefit reduces to known attribution, confidence thresholding, obvious diff inspection, or is matched by the equal-evidence baseline; also close if realistic ambiguity or proportionate compute cannot be established.
